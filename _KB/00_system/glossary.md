# Wine and Store Glossary

Use these plain-language definitions when a customer asks what a term means, or when the agent needs to use a term in a reply. Each entry gives a **customer-friendly line** to use first and a **note** for accuracy.

## A. Taste and structure

| Term | Customer-friendly line | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Dry** | "Not sweet: little to no sugar left after fermentation." | Customers often use "dry" to mean tannic, astringent or sophisticated. Always clarify (see [taste-discovery-conversation-flow.md](../01_wine-by-taste/taste-discovery-conversation-flow.md)). Most table wine is dry. |
| **Off-dry** | "A touch of sweetness, but not a dessert wine." | Roughly 10–30 g/L residual sugar in still wine; acidity makes it taste less sweet. |
| **Sweet** | "Noticeably sweet, like a dessert wine or Moscato." | |
| **Residual sugar (RS)** | "The natural grape sugar left in the wine." | Measured in grams per liter (g/L). |
| **Body** | "How heavy the wine feels in your mouth: skim milk (light) vs whole milk (full)." | Driven by alcohol, extract, sugar, glycerol. |
| **Tannin** | "The drying, grippy feeling, like strong black tea." | From grape skins, seeds, stems and oak. Mainly in reds. |
| **Acidity** | "The mouthwatering freshness, like a squeeze of lemon." | High acid = crisp/zesty; low acid = soft/round. |
| **Oak / oaky** | "Flavors of vanilla, toast, spice or butterscotch from aging in oak barrels." | New oak gives stronger flavor than old/neutral oak. Stainless steel = no oak flavor. |
| **Buttery** | "Rich, creamy texture and flavor, classic in some Chardonnays." | Mainly from malolactic conversion (diacetyl) plus oak and lees. |
| **Fruit-forward** | "Fruit flavors lead, like ripe berries or tropical fruit." | Common in warmer New World regions. |
| **Earthy / savory** | "Flavors like mushroom, leather, herbs or forest floor rather than just fruit." | Common in Old World and aged wines. |
| **Finish** | "How long the flavor lingers after you swallow." | |
| **Minerality** | "A stony, saline or flinty impression, not fruity." | Widely used, scientifically debated; describe as a style impression. |
| **Smooth** | Customer word. Usually means low tannin, low acidity, no harshness, often a little fruity. | Map to tannin=smooth, acidity=soft/fresh. |

## B. Production

| Term | Customer-friendly line | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Varietal / grape variety** | "The type of grape, e.g., Merlot or Riesling." | US label rule: a varietal name requires at least 75% of that grape (some states stricter: Oregon 90% for most varieties). |
| **Blend** | "Made from more than one grape." | E.g., Bordeaux blend, GSM (Grenache-Syrah-Mourvèdre). |
| **Vintage** | "The year the grapes were harvested." | US (TTB): at least 95% from the stated year if the label names an AVA; at least 85% if it names a state or county. NV = non-vintage, a blend of years (common in Champagne). |
| **Reserve / Reserva** | "Usually the producer's higher-tier wine." | No legal meaning in the US. In Spain (Reserva) and Italy (Riserva) there are legal minimum aging rules. |
| **Old vines (Vieilles Vignes)** | "Made from older vines, often more concentrated." | No legal definition. |
| **Malolactic conversion** | "A natural process that softens acidity and can add buttery notes." | |
| **Lees aging (sur lie)** | "Resting on spent yeast, which adds creaminess and bread-like notes." | |
| **Fortified wine** | "Wine with added grape spirit, like Port or Sherry: stronger and often sweeter." | Typically 15–22% ABV. |
| **Orange / skin-contact wine** | "White grapes made like a red, on their skins, giving amber color and a bit of grip." | |
| **Pét-nat** | "Naturally sparkling wine bottled before fermentation finishes; casual and often cloudy." | Méthode ancestrale. |
| **Dealcoholized** | "Regular wine with the alcohol removed." | US: <0.5% ABV to use the term without qualification (FDA). |

## C. Labels and places

| Term | Customer-friendly line | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Appellation** | "The officially defined place the grapes came from." | AVA (US), AOC/AOP (France), DOC/DOCG (Italy), DO/DOCa (Spain), etc. See [region-taxonomy-and-label-terms.md](../02_wine-by-region/region-taxonomy-and-label-terms.md). |
| **AVA** | "American Viticultural Area, an official US wine region like Napa Valley." | 85% of grapes from the AVA. |
| **Old World / New World** | "Traditional European regions vs. newer ones like the US, Australia, Chile." | A style shorthand: Old World is often more savory and higher-acid; New World often riper and fruitier. Many exceptions. |
| **Cru / Grand Cru / Premier Cru** | "A recognized top vineyard or village." | Meaning differs by region (Burgundy vs Bordeaux vs Champagne vs Alsace). |
| **Estate / Estate bottled** | "Grown and made by the same winery." | US: has legal requirements (winery in the AVA, controls the vineyards). |
| **Négociant** | "A producer that buys grapes or wine from growers." | Common in Burgundy and Champagne (NM). |
| **Grower Champagne (RM)** | "Made by the family that grows the grapes." | RM = Récoltant-Manipulant on the label. |

## D. Sparkling

| Term | Customer-friendly line |
|---|---|
| **Traditional method (Méthode Champenoise)** | "Second fermentation in the bottle. That's how Champagne, Cava and Crémant are made." |
| **Tank / Charmat method** | "Second fermentation in a big tank. Fresher and fruitier; that's how Prosecco is made." |
| **Brut** | "Dry, the most common sparkling style." See [sparkling-sweetness-scale.md](../04_sparkling-champagne-prosecco/sparkling-sweetness-scale.md). |
| **Extra Dry** | "Confusingly, a little *sweeter* than Brut." |
| **Blanc de Blancs / Blanc de Noirs** | "White wine from white grapes (Chardonnay) / white wine from red grapes (Pinot Noir, Meunier)." |

## E. Store terms

| Term | Customer-friendly line |
|---|---|
| **Fulfilling store** | "The licensed store that ships or delivers your order: our NJ store for NJ addresses, our NY store for NY addresses." |
| **Pre-arrival / futures** | "Wine you buy now that arrives later, often months later." |
| **Allocated** | "Limited-quantity wine with a per-customer cap." |
| **Mixed-case discount** | "A discount when you buy 6 or more eligible bottles, any mix." |
| **Vintage-specific listing** | "You'll get exactly the vintage shown, never a substitute without asking you." |
| **Adult signature** | "Someone 21+ with ID has to sign. We can't leave it at the door." |
| **Restocking fee** | "A partial charge on non-defective returns (NJ store only)." |
| **Final sale** | "Can't be returned unless it's damaged or defective." |
