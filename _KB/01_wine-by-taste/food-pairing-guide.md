# Food and Wine Pairing Guide

## 1. Pairing principles (explain simply)

1. **Match weight.** Light food with light wine; rich food with fuller wine.
2. **Acid loves acid and fat.** Tomato sauce, vinaigrettes and citrus need a high-acid wine. Acidity also cuts through fried and creamy food.
3. **Tannin loves fat and protein.** Grippy reds soften next to steak and hard cheese; they clash with oily fish (metallic taste) and spicy heat.
4. **Sweet with sweet.** Wine should be as sweet as or sweeter than dessert.
5. **Spice wants sweetness and low alcohol.** Off-dry Riesling or Gewürztraminer; avoid high-alcohol, oaky, tannic wines with chili heat.
6. **Salt loves bubbles and sweetness.** Sparkling wine with fried and salty snacks; Port or Sauternes with blue cheese.
7. **"What grows together goes together."** Regional pairings: Chianti and Tuscan food; Muscadet and oysters; Rioja and lamb.
8. **When unsure, pick sparkling, dry rosé or Pinot Noir**: the most food-flexible wines.

## 3. Holiday and cultural meal guidance

| Occasion | Suggestions |
|---|---|
| Thanksgiving | Pinot Noir, Cru Beaujolais, Zinfandel, off-dry Riesling, sparkling to start. Plan ~1 bottle per 2 adults for dinner (see [sparkling-occasions-gifting-and-handling.md](../04_sparkling-champagne-prosecco/sparkling-occasions-gifting-and-handling.md)). |
| Christmas / prime rib | Bordeaux, Napa Cabernet, Rioja Gran Reserva; Champagne for toasts. |
| Feast of the Seven Fishes | Prosecco, Vermentino, Pinot Grigio, Soave, Verdicchio; light red for heavier dishes. |
| Passover | Kosher-for-Passover wines. Use `search_products(is_kosher=true)` and check the "Kosher for Passover" designation in product details (not all kosher wine is KfP). |
| Lunar New Year | Off-dry Riesling, sparkling, Pinot Noir; red packaging/labels are popular for gifts. |
| Diwali | Off-dry whites, aromatic whites, sparkling. Many guests may prefer non-alcoholic options (see folder 07). |
| Valentine's | Rosé Champagne/sparkling, Pinot Noir, Brachetto with chocolate. |
| Summer BBQ | Dry rosé, Zinfandel, Malbec, chillable light reds, Prosecco. |

## 4. How the agent uses pairings

1. Identify dish → look up first choice + one alternative.
2. Combine with customer constraints (budget, sweetness, red/white preference).
3. `search_products` with the mapped style tag or grapes.
4. In the reply, explain the pairing in one short reason ("the acidity matches the tomato sauce").

## 5. Edge cases

- **Customer insists on red with fish**: support it. Suggest low-tannin reds (Pinot Noir, Gamay).
- **Dietary/religious requirements** (kosher, vegan): use product attributes; don't assume. Vegan status depends on fining agents (see folder 06).
- **Cooking with wine**: suggest an inexpensive dry wine of the style they'd drink. Avoid "cooking wine" with added salt; we generally don't sell those as table wine.
- **Pairing for someone who doesn't drink**: point to non-alcoholic options (folder 07).
