# Rich & Full-Bodied White Wines

## 1. What they are

Rich whites are **dry, medium- to full-bodied, with a round, creamy texture**, often from oak aging, lees contact and malolactic conversion. Customers say "buttery", "creamy", "oaky", "smooth white", "Chardonnay like Rombauer", or "a white for winter".

**Catalog:** `style_tag=rich-white` · `body in (medium, full)`, `acidity in (soft, fresh)`, `oak in (subtle, pronounced)` (some rich whites are unoaked but textured, e.g., Viognier).

## 2. Key wines

| Wine | Taste in plain words | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **California Chardonnay (oaked)** | Butter, vanilla, baked apple, tropical fruit, toast | The classic "buttery Chardonnay". Sonoma, Napa, Central Coast, Russian River. Styles range from very rich to more balanced. |
| **White Burgundy (Meursault, Puligny-/Chassagne-Montrachet)** | Hazelnut, lemon cream, toast, minerality | Premium. More restrained than California. |
| **Mâcon / Pouilly-Fuissé** | Apple, citrus, gentle cream | Value white Burgundy, lightly oaked or unoaked. |
| **Viognier** | Apricot, honeysuckle, oily texture, low acidity | Unoaked richness. |
| **White Rhône blends (Marsanne, Roussanne, Grenache Blanc)** | Pear, honey, almond, herbs | Côtes du Rhône Blanc (value), Châteauneuf-du-Pape Blanc (premium). |
| **White Rioja (aged, Viura)** | Nutty, oxidative, honey, spice | Traditional producers age it for years. |
| **Oaked Sauvignon Blanc / Fumé Blanc, White Bordeaux (Sémillon blend)** | Citrus plus creamy, toasty, waxy | Pessac-Léognan for premium. |
| **Chenin Blanc (South Africa, oaked)** | Quince, honey, toast | Value premium. |
| **Australian Chardonnay (modern)** | Fresher, with nutty, struck-match notes | Often less buttery than in the past. |

## 3. Indicative price bands

- **Everyday:** Big-brand California Chardonnays, Côtes du Rhône Blanc, Mâcon-Villages.
- **Step-up:** Sonoma Coast/Russian River Chardonnay, Pouilly-Fuissé, Viognier.
- **Premium:** Napa Chardonnay icons, Meursault village, White Châteauneuf.
- **Luxury/Collectible:** Puligny/Chassagne Premier Cru, Grand Cru Burgundy (Corton-Charlemagne, Bâtard-Montrachet).

## 4. How to recommend

- **"Buttery Chardonnay"**: `grape=Chardonnay`, `oak=pronounced`, `region_path` California first.
- **"Chardonnay but not too oaky"**: `oak=subtle`, Burgundy/Mâcon/Sonoma Coast/cool-climate.
- **"Rich but no oak"**: Viognier, Marsanne/Roussanne, unoaked Chenin.
- **Winter whites / holiday dinner**: rich whites with roast turkey, lobster, creamy pasta.
- If someone dislikes "buttery", check [light-crisp-whites.md](light-crisp-whites.md) (Chablis).

## 5. Food pairing

Lobster with butter, crab cakes, roast chicken and turkey, pork loin, creamy pastas (carbonara, Alfredo), risotto, corn chowder, brie and triple-cream cheeses. Serve lightly chilled: 50–55°F (10–13°C). Too cold hides the flavor.

## 6. Edge cases

- **"I don't like Chardonnay"** usually means "I don't like heavy oak/butter". Offer unoaked Chardonnay (Chablis) before ruling out the grape entirely.
- **Premature oxidation in older white Burgundy** (roughly 1995–2010 vintages) is a known industry issue. For claims, see [corked-or-flawed-wine.md](../12_returns-refunds-damages/corked-or-flawed-wine.md) and the older-vintage exception (A-33).
- **Natural deposits** (tartrate crystals, "wine diamonds") in whites are harmless, not glass. They are a common source of complaints; explain kindly.

> **Agent:** Lovely choice with turkey. Here are three rich, oaky California Chardonnays near your budget: …
