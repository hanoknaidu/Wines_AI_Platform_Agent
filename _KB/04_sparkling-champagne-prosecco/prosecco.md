# Prosecco

## 1. What Prosecco is

Prosecco is an Italian sparkling wine from **northeastern Italy (Veneto and Friuli Venezia Giulia)**, made mostly from the **Glera** grape (at least 85%), usually by the **tank (Charmat/Martinotti) method**. It's fresh, fruity and floral: green apple, pear, white peach, honeysuckle. Bubbles are lighter and frothier than Champagne. Prosecco is best **young**, generally within 1–2 years of vintage.

## 2. Quality pyramid

| Tier | Area | Notes for the customer |
|---|---|---|
| **Prosecco DOC** | Large area across 9 provinces of Veneto and Friuli; "Prosecco DOC Treviso" and "Trieste" sub-mentions | Most Prosecco sold in the US; fresh, easy, great value; perfect for mimosas and spritzes |
| **Asolo Prosecco Superiore DOCG** | Hills around Asolo | Hillside quality tier |
| **Conegliano Valdobbiadene Prosecco Superiore DOCG** | Steep hills between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene (UNESCO World Heritage landscape since 2019) | More aromatic, finer bubbles, more mineral. A worthwhile step up for sipping |
| **Rive** (within Conegliano Valdobbiadene DOCG) | Single steep hillside villages/sub-zones | Site-specific, often vintage-dated |
| **Superiore di Cartizze DOCG** | A tiny ~100-hectare hill near Valdobbiadene | Top of the pyramid; often slightly sweeter (Dry style); premium |

**Prosecco Rosé DOC** (since the 2020 vintage): Glera with 10–15% Pinot Nero, minimum 60 days in tank, always vintage-dated ("Millesimato"). Light, strawberry-tinged.

**Col Fondo / Sui Lieviti:** traditional bottle-fermented, unfiltered, cloudy, dry Prosecco. Niche, "pét-nat-like".

## 3. Prosecco sweetness labels

Prosecco uses the standard sparkling terms, and many popular Proseccos are **Extra Dry**, which is *sweeter* than Brut:

| Label | Residual sugar (g/L) | Taste |
|---|---|---|
| Brut Nature | 0–3 | Bone dry (rare) |
| Extra Brut | 0–6 | Very dry |
| **Brut** | 0–12 | Dry, crisp |
| **Extra Dry** | 12–17 | Off-dry, softly fruity (most common style for many brands) |
| **Dry** | 17–32 | Noticeably sweet (common for Cartizze) |
| Demi-Sec | 32–50 | Sweet (rare) |

See [sparkling-sweetness-scale.md](sparkling-sweetness-scale.md).

## 4. Frizzante vs Spumante

- **Spumante:** fully sparkling (most Prosecco), mushroom cork with cage.
- **Frizzante:** lightly sparkling, sometimes a string-tied or regular cork; also "Tranquillo" (still) exists.

## 5. Indicative price bands

- **Everyday (< $15):** most Prosecco DOC brands.
- **Step-up ($15–30):** Conegliano Valdobbiadene DOCG, Asolo DOCG, quality DOC Rosé, Rive.
- **Premium ($30+):** Cartizze, top Rive bottlings.

## 6. How to recommend

| Need | Pick | Filters |
|---|---|---|
| Mimosas, Bellinis, brunch for a crowd | Prosecco DOC Brut or Extra Dry | `appellation=Prosecco DOC`, `price_max=15` |
| Aperol / Hugo spritz | Prosecco DOC Brut (less sugar works better with bitter aperitivo) | `sweetness_label=Brut` |
| Toast, sipping, gift | Conegliano Valdobbiadene DOCG / Rive | `appellation=Conegliano Valdobbiadene` |
| "Not too dry" bubbly | Prosecco Extra Dry or Dry | `sweetness_label in (Extra Dry, Dry)` |
| Pink bubbles on a budget | Prosecco Rosé DOC | `style_tag=sparkling`, `color=rose` |
| Wedding / large party | Prosecco DOC in quantity; mention the mixed-case discount (A-50) | — |

## 7. Edge cases

- **Vintage on Prosecco:** many are "Millesimato" (≥ 85% from that year). Freshness matters more than vintage prestige. Under $25, adjacent vintage substitution is automatic (A-22); the newer vintage is generally preferable.
- **"Is Prosecco cheap Champagne?"** No. It's a different style from a different place and method, prized for freshness, not toastiness.
- **Prosecco vs Moscato:** Moscato d'Asti is much sweeter and lower in alcohol.
- **Bottle stored too long:** Prosecco can fade after 2–3 years. If a customer reports a "flat, tired" old Prosecco from their own cellar, that's ageing, not a defect.
