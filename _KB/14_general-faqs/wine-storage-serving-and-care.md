# Wine Storage, Serving and Care

## 1. Storing unopened wine

| Factor | Ideal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | **~55°F (13°C)**, steady; anything 45–65°F is fine short term | Heat ages wine fast and can push corks; swings are worse than a steady slightly-warm temperature |
| Light | Dark | UV light can damage wine ("light strike"), especially in clear bottles |
| Humidity | ~50–70% | Keeps natural corks from drying (mainly for long-term storage) |
| Position | **On their side** for cork-sealed wines kept for months+; screw caps can stand | Keeps the cork moist |
| Vibration | Minimal | — |
| Avoid | Top of the fridge, near the stove, garage/attic, car trunk in summer/winter | Heat and temperature swings |

**Most wines are made to drink within 1–5 years.** Only a minority (top Bordeaux, Barolo, vintage Port, some Riesling, Champagne prestige cuvées) benefit from long aging. Kitchen-fridge storage is fine for a few weeks, but it is too dry and cold long term.

## 2. Serving temperatures

| Style | Temperature | Shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Sparkling, light whites, rosé | 43–50°F (6–10°C) | Fridge 2–3 h; ice bucket 20 min |
| Fuller whites (oaked Chardonnay) | 50–55°F (10–13°C) | Out of the fridge 15–20 min before |
| Light reds (Pinot, Beaujolais) | 55–60°F (13–15°C) | 15–20 min in the fridge |
| Medium/full reds | 60–65°F (16–18°C) | 10–15 min in the fridge if the room is warm |
| Dessert/fortified | 45–60°F depending on style | — |

## 3. Opening and decanting

- **Corkscrew tips:** cut the foil below the lip; insert the worm in the center; ease the cork out. For old, fragile corks, use a two-prong (Ah-So) opener.
- **Broken cork:** push it in and strain the wine, or decant through a fine strainer. Not a defect claim unless the cork was faulty and the wine is spoiled.
- **Sparkling:** safety steps in [sparkling-occasions-gifting-and-handling.md](../04_sparkling-champagne-prosecco/sparkling-occasions-gifting-and-handling.md).
- **Decanting:**
  - Young, tannic reds (Cabernet, Barolo, Syrah): 30–90 min to soften.
  - Old reds/vintage Port: stand the bottle upright 24 h, then decant gently to leave sediment; drink soon after.
  - Reductive wines (struck-match smell): a quick decant helps.

## 4. Leftover wine (after opening)

| Wine | Keeps (recorked, in the fridge) |
|---|---|
| Sparkling (with a sparkling stopper) | 1–3 days |
| Light whites and rosé | 3–5 days |
| Full whites | 3–5 days |
| Reds | 3–5 days (let them warm slightly before drinking) |
| Boxed wine | ~4–6 weeks after opening |
| Fortified: Ruby/LBV Port | ~4–6 weeks |
| Tawny Port | ~1–2 months |
| Fino/Manzanilla Sherry | 1 week (refrigerate) |
| Madeira | Months |
| Non-alcoholic wine | 2–5 days |

Tips: re-cork or use a stopper; refrigerate reds too; vacuum pumps and argon sprays extend life a little.

## 5. Common care questions

- **"There are crystals in my wine."** Tartrates: harmless and natural (see [corked-or-flawed-wine.md](../12_returns-refunds-damages/corked-or-flawed-wine.md)).
- **"Can I freeze wine?"** For cooking, yes (ice-cube trays). Bottles in the freezer can burst or push the cork; don't forget them.
- **"Can wine go bad unopened?"** Yes, with heat damage, cork failure, or simply age beyond its window. Most everyday wines are best within a few years.
- **"Screw cap vs cork?"** Both are quality closures. Screw caps prevent cork taint and keep wine fresh; many top producers use them.
- **"How long can I keep the wine I bought in my car?"** Avoid it: car interiors get very hot or cold; take wine home promptly.

## 6. Sample dialogue

> **Customer:** I opened a bottle of Pinot last night. Is it still good?
> **Agent:** Most likely yes. Recorked and kept in the fridge, an opened red is usually good for 3–5 days. Take it out about 20 minutes before drinking so it's not too cold. If it smells vinegary or flat, it's past its best.
